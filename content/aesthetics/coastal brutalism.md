## what it is

A visual language that pairs the heaviness of Brutalist architecture w/ softness and openness of coastal environments.

It is defined by 
raw concrete, blocky geometric forms, deep shadows and exposed structure, set against water, sky, salt air and sparse landscapes. The tension is what makes it interesting: buildings feel monumental and severe, while the surrounding environment feels calm, bright and expansive...

## what I like about it

- the contrast between concrete and nature feels really satisfying
- heavy forms can still feel peaceful when surrounded by light and water
- the materials feel honest and tactile
- the architecture feels dramatic without needing decoration
- I like how weathering becomes part of the aesthetic
- there is a quietness to it despite how massive the forms are
- it feels both harsh and serene at the same time

## recurring elements

- raw concrete
- exposed structure
- large geometric volumes
- spirals
- deep shadows
- open terraces
- ocean or harbour views
- weathered surfaces
- sparse landscaping

- sculptural staircases
- wind, salt and sun visibly affecting materials

## where I see it

- coastal houses
- seaside civic architecture
- Mediterranean concrete buildings
- Australian coastal architecture
- cliffside homes
- pavilions and lookouts
- modernist beach houses
- resorts with raw material palettes
- architecture photography focused on light and shadow
